\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a systematic study of knowledge conflicts in multi-hop question answering, evaluating four LLMs across two datasets, three conflict types, and over 19,000 examples. Our main findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item A single conflict at the answer-bearing hop reduces accuracy by 40--52 percentage points on 2-hop and 44--47pp on 3-hop chains, with all comparisons statistically significant ($p < 0.0001$, large effect sizes).

    \item In both 2-hop and 3-hop reasoning, a strong asymmetry exists: conflicts at the answer-bearing hop are far more damaging than conflicts at bridge hops. Bridge-hop CFR is approximately 2\%, while answer-hop CFR reaches 27--36\%.

    \item In 3-hop chains, this asymmetry is most extreme: early-hop conflicts cause minimal damage, while final-hop conflicts cause near-total failure (Gemini drops to 0.5\%). This reveals that models are disproportionately dependent on the final-answer document, raising concerns about the faithfulness of multi-step reasoning.

    \item Model size predicts absolute robustness, though all models suffer comparable answer-hop drops (44--52pp). Reasoning models (Qwen3-32B) and Gemini show the highest context following rates ($\sim$36\%), a double-edged property.

    \item Temporal conflicts are the most disruptive (50.8pp average drop, CFR up to 80\%), followed by numerical (36.2pp) and factual (26.6pp). Entity popularity shows a directional influence on conflict resolution strategy.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Implications}

For RAG system design, our results suggest that \textbf{document quality control should prioritize the final-answer document} in multi-hop retrieval chains. Conflict detection mechanisms should focus particularly on temporal and numerical claims, where models follow fake answers 50--80\% of the time. The choice between larger and smaller models involves a tradeoff: larger models provide better absolute robustness but at higher cost, and reasoning models' tendency to follow context is beneficial only when that context is reliable.

\subsection{Limitations}

Our entity substitution approach produces relatively simple conflicts; real-world conflicts may be more subtle. We evaluate only English-language datasets (HotpotQA, MuSiQue) with bridge/compositional questions. The entity popularity analysis is limited by HotpotQA's skew toward obscure entities. API-based evaluation prevents analysis of model internals, and exact match may undercount semantically correct answers.

\subsection{Future Work}

Key directions include: (1)~conflict detection and mitigation methods; (2)~mechanistic analysis of conflict resolution through attention patterns; (3)~extension to 4+ hop chains to test the generality of the final-hop vulnerability; (4)~multi-conflict scenarios with simultaneous injections at multiple hops; and (5)~conflict-aware fine-tuning to improve robustness without sacrificing baseline performance.

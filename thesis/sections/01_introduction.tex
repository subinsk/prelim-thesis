\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large Language Models (LLMs) acquire extensive world knowledge during pre-training, storing factual associations within their parameters---\emph{parametric knowledge}---which is inherently static and cannot be updated without retraining. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)~\citep{lewis2020retrieval,guu2020realm} addresses this by supplementing LLMs with external documents at inference time, but a critical failure mode arises when retrieved context \emph{contradicts} parametric knowledge---a \emph{knowledge conflict}~\citep{longpre2021entity,pan2023risk}. The model must implicitly decide whether to trust parametric memory or defer to context, with significant implications for system reliability.

This problem is substantially more complex in \emph{multi-hop reasoning}, where answering a question requires synthesising information across multiple documents~\citep{yang2018hotpotqa,trivedi2022musique}. A conflict at any single hop can propagate through the chain, corrupting downstream inferences. Existing knowledge-conflict studies~\citep{longpre2021entity,xie2024adaptive,chen2022rich} have focused primarily on single-hop settings; the interaction between conflicts and hop position in multi-step chains remains poorly understood.

This work addresses this gap through a systematic experimental study in which controlled conflicts are injected at specific hop positions and four LLMs are evaluated across two multi-hop datasets. Six research questions structure the analysis:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textbf{RQ1}] How significantly do knowledge conflicts degrade multi-hop QA accuracy?
    \item[\textbf{RQ2}] Does the hop position of conflict injection affect degradation in 2-hop reasoning?
    \item[\textbf{RQ3}] How does error propagation differ between 2-hop and 3-hop chains?
    \item[\textbf{RQ4}] Does model size predict robustness to conflicts?
    \item[\textbf{RQ5}] Does the conflict type (factual, temporal, numerical) influence severity?
    \item[\textbf{RQ6}] Does entity popularity affect conflict resolution strategy?
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} This work contributes: (1)~a controlled conflict-injection framework for multi-hop QA; (2)~a large-scale evaluation across 4 models, 2 datasets, and 3 conflict types totalling 19,416 examples; (3)~the finding that answer-bearing hop conflicts cause 40--52pp drops while bridge-hop conflicts are far less damaging---a pattern consistent across 2-hop and 3-hop chains; (4)~a conflict-type taxonomy showing temporal conflicts are the most disruptive (50.8pp drop, CFR up to 80\%); and (5)~evidence that entity popularity monotonically influences conflict resolution.

\begin{center}
{\large\textbf{Abstract}}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.5cm}

\noindent
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems supplement Large Language Models (LLMs) with external context, but what happens when that context contradicts the model's parametric knowledge? We present a systematic study of \emph{knowledge conflicts} in multi-hop question answering, evaluating how four LLMs---Llama-3.3-70B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-32B, and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite---handle contradictions injected at specific positions within 2-hop (HotpotQA) and 3-hop (MuSiQue) reasoning chains. Across 19,416 evaluations, we find that: (1)~a single conflict at the answer-bearing hop reduces accuracy by 40--52 percentage points ($p < 0.0001$, $h > 0.8$); (2)~in both 2-hop and 3-hop chains, answer-bearing hop conflicts are far more damaging than bridge-hop conflicts (CFR 27--36\% vs.\ $\sim$2\%); (3)~in 3-hop chains, conflicts at the final hop cause catastrophic failure (44--47pp drops across all models), while early-hop conflicts cause minimal degradation; (4)~temporal conflicts are the most disruptive (50.8pp average drop, CFR up to 80\%); and (5)~entity popularity influences whether models defer to context or override with parametric memory. Our findings reveal a critical vulnerability in multi-hop RAG pipelines: models are disproportionately dependent on the final-answer document in reasoning chains.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} knowledge conflicts, multi-hop reasoning, retrieval-augmented generation, large language models, question answering
